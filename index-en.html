Sources: pappers.fr (record updated 6 September 2026), Reuters, European Commission. Screenshot taken 6 September 2026.

Claude Code — company-register search and screenshot
This is our first surprise, spend time here. The French entity bills €12.9 m and calls itself a PR consultancy. It does not sell the advertising: that is invoiced from Ireland. So when a French brand buys ads on X, almost none of that money lands in France, and the local team is a 20-to-49-people relay. Say the sentence: “The name never changed because the name was never the product.” 70 seconds.

Stop 2 — Personas

Who is there — and who you actually want

Persona A — the user X really has

Théo, 27, Lille. Junior developer.

  • Opens X 5 to 8 times a day, seven minutes total, almost always on mobile.
  • Reads, does not post. Follows news, tech, football, memes.
  • Arrives direct, never through search. Bookmarks instead of liking.
  • Blocks ads mentally: he is there for the timeline, not for brands.

What he is worth to a brand: reach, almost no conversion.

Persona B — the user a brand should target

Camille, 34, Paris. Journalist / industry insider.

  • Posts and quotes, 200 to 2 000 followers but the right ones.
  • Uses X as a professional monitoring tool: sources, competitors, live events.
  • Relays what is useful or funny within the hour, or never.
  • One repost from her is worth a thousand impressions bought.

What she is worth: credibility and second-degree reach. This is who you write for.

Personas built from the audience data above (Ecommerce Nation, SimilarWeb, Statista). Not a survey: a reading of the figures.

Stop 3 — Tips

Launching on X: what we would actually do

1 · Post daily, or don't start. One to three posts a day. On a network built on freshness, a weekly rhythm is invisible.

2 · Reply before you publish. Spend the first month answering others. Replies cost nothing and are how new accounts are actually discovered.

3 · Text first, image second. X is the only major network where a naked sentence still outperforms a designed visual. Keep the image for proof, not decoration.

4 · One voice, not a brand voice. Burger King France follows zero accounts and writes like a person. It works because it is consistent, not because it is funny.

5 · Ride the event, never the calendar. A post published 40 minutes after a match is worth ten posts planned a month ahead.

6 · Measure bookmarks and replies, not likes. Likes are polite. Bookmarks mean useful, replies mean alive.

Built from the four objectives listed by Ecommerce Nation (interact, react to events, adopt a different tone, share news) and from the accounts observed on 6 September 2026.

Stop 3 — Use cases

Use X for this. Never for that.

Use it

  • Live events — matches, keynotes, launches, crises.
  • Public customer service — answering in the open builds more trust than a form.
  • Tech and B2B credibility — reaching journalists, developers, investors.
  • Monitoring — competitors and sector news, for free, in real time.
  • Tone experiments — the one place a brand is allowed to be dry.

Don't

  • Mass reach in France — 15.5 M reachable and falling. Use Instagram or TikTok.
  • Selling directly — no native shop, weak conversion. Ecommerce Nation says it plainly: don't make commercial posts.
  • Reaching women over 45 — structurally the wrong audience.
  • Beautiful visual storytelling — the feed is text-shaped, images get cropped.
  • Brand safety-sensitive sectors — moderation is contested, a €120 m fine says so.

Sources: Ecommerce Nation 2024–2025 · European Commission, DSA decision, December 2025.

The right-hand column is what will be remembered. Say the last line out loud: “If your brand cannot afford to appear next to anything, X is not a channel, it is a risk.” 45 seconds.

Stop 5 — Three accounts to follow

Three accounts, three inventions

@OpenAI — 5.2 M followers.
Uses X as its press room: products are announced here first, in plain text, no press release. The invention is the format — a launch is a post.

@grok — 9 M followers, 39 M posts.
An AI that is a native user of the network: you mention it in a thread and it answers publicly. The invention is that the tool lives inside the feed, not beside it.

@BurgerKingFR — 188.7 K followers, 0 following.
A bio written as an ID card, posts written like a person. The invention is discipline: the same voice since 2013, and it follows nobody.

Screenshots taken from the platform on 6 September 2026 (@grok, @BurgerKingFR) and in August 2026 (@OpenAI).

Claude Code — captured the three profiles live
Burger King is the one to develop, it is the actionable one for a French brand. Zero following is not a detail: it is a statement that the account is a broadcaster, not a community member — the opposite of every social media textbook, and it works. Ask the room: would you dare recommend that to a client? 60 seconds.

Stop 8 — Conclusion

X stopped being a social network. Nobody has renamed the job yet.

Our position. X is no longer a place to be seen. It is a place to be quoted. Fifteen million reachable people in France, seven minutes a day, but 12 minutes 15 per visit worldwide and 70 % direct traffic: this is a habit, not an audience. A brand should stop measuring reach on X and start measuring who repeats it.

What we would tell a client tomorrow. Keep the account, cut the budget, move the money to Instagram, and keep one person able to answer within the hour. On X, response time is the whole strategy.

And now — the opening

X was bought by an AI lab, then merged into a rocket company. Grok already answers inside the feed. So the real question for 2027 is not whether X keeps its users.

It is whether the next X still needs them. If the timeline can be written and answered by AI, a social network without a crowd is no longer a contradiction — it is a product.

That is the question we would like to hear the class argue about.

No summary, as requested. Our reading of the figures presented above.
