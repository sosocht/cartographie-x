Twenty years in ten dates

Jack Dorsey, Biz Stone, Evan Williams and Dick Costolo at the 2013 stock market listing.

2006

Launched on 15 July. First message on 21 March: just setting up my twttr.

2007

The hashtag is invented by a user, to gather messages on the same topic.

2013

Stock market listing in New York.

2017

The limit moves to 280 characters.

2022

E. Musk buys Twitter for $44bn and takes it private.

2023

23 July: the bird becomes X. The Twitter name is dropped.

2024

17 May: twitter.com finally redirects to x.com.

2025

March: xAI, Elon Musk's artificial intelligence company, buys X for $33bn.

2025

December: €120m European fine for breaching the Digital Services Act.

2026

January: the feed algorithm is released as open source. February: xAI merges with SpaceX.

Keep only three of them out loud. 2006: the service was born inside Odeo, a struggling podcast company looking for a second product, so Twitter was a plan B. 2007: the hashtag was proposed by a user, Chris Messina, to gather messages on one topic into a single thread; the company adopted it afterwards. 2017: the limit doubled, and X is the only network ever to have changed its unit of writing. 2023: the rebranding gave up one of the best known names in the world, and the verb "to tweet" that came with it; the platform kept its users but has to rebuild its reputation on a single letter. 2025: xAI, Elon Musk's artificial intelligence company that builds Grok, buys X; the network becomes a source of data and audience to train and distribute models. If asked about the fine: in its decision of 5 December 2025, the European Commission concluded that the blue tick misled users, since it can be bought and proves no identity, and that the platform failed its transparency obligations under the Digital Services Act, the European law of 2022. X is appealing, but in the meantime it no longer speaks of "verified" accounts in Europe, only "Premium" accounts. If asked about 2026: xAI merged with SpaceX, Musk's rocket company, so X now sits inside a group whose business is space and artificial intelligence, not social networking. 60 seconds.

Two ideas, one per column. On the left: X is no longer a company, it is a nesting doll. The network has belonged to xAI since March 2025, and xAI merged with SpaceX in February 2026. Explain why there are two. The audience you have is not the one you write for. On X the passive majority is Théo, but all the value sits with Camille, because X is a relay network, not a reach network. Show the gauges: Théo does not take part and does not buy, Camille posts and passes on. Then the line: "On X you do not talk to your audience, you talk to the people who will repeat you." 60 seconds.

Getting started on X: our six recommendations

01 Post every day, or do not start

One to three messages a day.

02 Reply before you post

Month 1: reply to posts.

03 Text first, image second

Virality: image < text.

04 Originality

Burger King France follows no account and writes like a person.

05 Follow the event, never the calendar

One message posted 40 minutes after a match is worth ten scheduled a month ahead.

06 Measure bookmarks and replies

A like = polite, a bookmark = useful, a reply = alive.

This is the practical part, the lecturer asked for it explicitly. Say points 1, 2 and 6 out loud, they are the counter-intuitive ones. Push "reply before you post": it costs nothing, and nobody does it. On the two feeds: "For you" is ranked by the algorithm, "Following" is chronological and only contains accounts you follow. The score adds up predicted probabilities of action, not counts of likes. Three corrections follow the ranking: author diversity, an out-of-network discount, and a boost for accounts with few impressions. 60 seconds.

The pros and cons of X

Worth doing
  • +Live events: matches, conferences, launches, crises.
  • +Public customer service.
  • +Tech and B2B credibility: journalists, developers, investors.
  • +Market monitoring.
  • +Tone: frank and direct ≠ LinkedIn.
  • +Publishing faster than anywhere else.
  • +An adult, working audience.
To rule out
  • –Falling user base in France: ↘ 15.5M.
  • –Direct selling: no native store and weak conversion.
  • –Women over 45.
  • –A risk-free feed: fake accounts, crypto scams and automation sit next to the brands.
  • –Replacing LinkedIn: prospecting, recruiting, commercial relationships.

Sources: Ecommerce Nation (accessed 06/09/2026), 2024 and 2025 guides · European Commission (accessed 06/09/2026), DSA decision of December 2025.

The right-hand column is the one people will remember. To develop out loud: public customer service inspires more trust than a form, because the answer is visible to everyone. Monitoring covers competitors and the sector, free and in real time. An adult, working audience, where TikTok and Instagram aim first at the general public. Polished visual storytelling has no place here, the feed is built for text and crops images. And for sectors that care about brand safety, moderation is contested, the €120m fine proves it: "If your brand cannot afford to appear next to anything at all, X is not a channel, it is a risk." 45 seconds.

Develop Burger King, it is the case a French brand can actually act on. Following nobody is not a detail, it is a statement: the account is a broadcaster, not a member of the community. It is the opposite of every social media manual, and it works. OpenAI turned X into its newsroom: a product launch became a post. Grok is an artificial intelligence that lives in the feed and answers when mentioned. Put the question to the room: would you dare recommend following nobody to a client? 60 seconds.
